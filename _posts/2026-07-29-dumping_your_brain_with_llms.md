In [the previous blog post](2026-07-02-thoughts-toward-ai-native-development-process.md),
I described how context matters to get quality output from LLMs and AI tools.
As a next step, I've lately been writing down my domain knowledge about the
system and softwares I'm working on. It's required to delegate more tasks for
AI tools.

When we work on software development tasks (Although I don't believe it's only
about software development, I don't want to expand the scope unnecessarily here.),
we leverages a lot of tacit knowledge. While it is sometimes articulated for
sharing, most of it trapped inside individual minds, and people often aren't
aware it exists. However, AI tools need it to do better jobs.

It's a daunting task to verbalize tacit knowledge as a logical document.
Even if we understand concepts and nuances in the brain, it easiy runs into a
problem to explain them in a natual language. It's difficult even in my mother
language (= Japanese). No need to say when it comes to English.
It is a reason that tacit knowledge largely never go out from individual minds.
However, AI tools also make the hurdle of verbalization much lower.

Tacit knowledge is more like intuitions rather than structured memory. Unless
we are intentional, it stays unorganized. To verbalize it, we need to find
words that match with nuances of the knowledge. This process takes long time,
but this is the point we can accelerate with AI tools. They are very fast to
expand short nuanced concepts into logical documents. The output may not often
perfect, but it's still better than knowledge stays only individual minds.

Verbalizing knowledge and thoughts is good to organize memory, and it also
sometimes leads to new findings. To be honest, I was surprised the amount of
domain knowledge I silently assumed on working on tasks when I wrote down it.

Are you concerned that the job security is threatened if you expose knowledge
only you know? I'm optimizstic about that point. If your knowledge is valuable,
you should be valued about the ability to produce that knowledge. Also,
knowledge is just information. It makes value when it's utilized to solve
real problems. Your skill in seamlessly applying domain expertise to complete
tasks should also be rewarded. I feel rather relaxed after writing down it
because I'm no longer the single person who knows it. I can now delegate the
responsibility for other members to perform tasks with leveraging the knowledge.

Dumping tacit knowledge is valuable for both you and others. AI tools support
the heavy lifting of verbalizing it. Allocating a small time for verbalization
improves your overall productivity for sure.
